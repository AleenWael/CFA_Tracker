How this tracker works (the method)
This follows the approach most prep providers recommend: roughly 300 hours of study, time weighted toward the heavy topics, practice questions straight after each module, and a final phase of full mock exams.
  1. Skim. Before a module, read the Learning Outcome Statements and the summary so you know what you'll be tested on.
  2. Study. Watch the video, read the summary and work through the examples. Open Components under a module to tick off its sub-topics one by one: ticking the last one marks the module studied.
  3. Quizzes. After each component, do its Kaplan module quiz and enter the score next to it. The module score is the average of its quizzes, and once every quiz has a score the module's Qs are done. Weak spots show the components you scored lowest on. 70%+ is on track, 60–69 needs a second look, and <60 is a weak area that gets priority in final review.
  4. Recall checks. 7 days after your practice Qs, and again 3 weeks later, a module shows up under Recall checks. Do 5–10 quick questions or explain the key formulas from memory. Spaced repetition like this is the cheapest way to stop early topics fading by February.
  5. Topic test. When a whole topic is finished, do 20–30 mixed questions on it and enter the score at the bottom of that topic.
  6. Revision, module by module. From 1 January every module comes back once (weakest first after a re-plan) as a Revision entry: re-read your notes and the summary, then do a CFA Institute practice set and a Kaplan practice set and enter both scores. Entering both, or ticking Revised, marks it done. The Review box on a module ticks the same entry.
  7. Mocks. Three full timed mocks (23 Jan, 6 Feb, 13 Feb), each followed by a debrief day, then a light last week. Candidates who score 70%+ on mocks pass at a very high rate.
Readiness is your scores weighted by each topic's share of the exam, so on the 2027 weights. Quant is now 11–14% (up from 6–9%), so a weak Quant score costs as much as a weak FSA one, and Ethics (10–15%) still outweighs Derivatives (6–9%). Use Re-plan remaining whenever you fall behind or get ahead: it spreads the unstudied modules evenly up to the review phase.
